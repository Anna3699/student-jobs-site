Know Your Rights

Work rights for international students

Understanding what you can and can't do legally is the foundation of your job search. Here's what you need to know as an international student in Australia.

⚠️
Important: Work hour limits apply during study

On a student visa (subclass 500) you are limited to 48 hours per fortnight while your course is in session. Breaching this limit is a serious visa condition violation and can result in your visa being cancelled.

📚 During Study Periods

  • Maximum 48 hours per fortnight (approx. 24 hrs/week)
  • Applies when your course is in session
  • Includes all paid employment — casual, part-time, volunteer with stipend
  • Your partner/spouse may also be subject to hour limits depending on visa subclass
  • Hours reset every fortnightly period — you cannot "carry over"

☀️ During Scheduled Breaks

  • Unlimited hours during official scheduled course breaks (e.g., summer holidays)
  • Break must be scheduled by your institution — not a personal leave period
  • Keep evidence of official semester dates in case you're ever asked
  • If your course ends, contact your institution about enrollment status
  • Great opportunity for full-time holiday work or internships

✅ Eligible Work Types

  • Casual hospitality, retail, admin roles
  • Paid internships and work placements
  • Part-time professional roles
  • Research assistant and tutoring roles
  • Freelance / ABN work (still counts toward 48-hour limit)

🚫 Restrictions to Know

  • You cannot work before your course commences
  • You cannot work if your visa has a "no work" condition (8105 condition)
  • Volunteering for a business that employs paid workers may count as work
  • Uber Eats, food delivery — counts toward the 48-hour limit
  • Check your visa grant letter for specific conditions
🎉
No work hour restrictions on the 485!

The Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485) allows you to work full-time with no restrictions. This visa is specifically designed to help you transition from study to work in Australia.

Plan your next visa

Use the 485 period to build PR-worthy experience for employer nomination or points-based visas.

💼 Employer-Sponsored Visas

  • TSS visa (482): Employer nominates you for a specific occupation. 2–4 years, pathway to PR
  • ENS visa (186): Permanent visa via employer nomination — requires 3 years work experience
  • RSMS (187): For regional employer nomination — leads directly to PR
  • Employer pays nomination and visa fees for these streams

🌟 Skilled Independent (189 & 190)

  • 189 Skilled Independent: Points-based, no employer/state needed. Score 65+ points
  • 190 Skilled Nominated: State nominates you — earns extra 5 points toward PR
  • Points scored on age, English, work experience, qualifications
  • Use the 485 visa to build Australian work experience for more points

🤝 Working Holiday Visa (417 / 462)

  • Available to citizens of eligible countries aged 18–30 (or 35 for some)
  • Work anywhere in Australia for 12 months, extendable to 3 years
  • 6-month work limit with any single employer
  • Popular for part-time and regional work while studying or after graduation

🎓 Post-Graduate Sponsorship

  • Many Graduate Programs are structured to sponsor you on a 482 TSS after your 485 expires
  • Big 4 banks, Big 4 consulting, major tech companies all commonly sponsor
  • Ask HR during the interview process about visa support policy
  • Some companies have a standard 2-year service requirement before sponsoring
ℹ️
Employers must check your work rights

Under Australian law, your employer is required to verify your work rights before engaging you. They will typically use VEVO (Visa Entitlement Verification Online) to do this.

🔍 What is VEVO?

  • Visa Entitlement Verification Online — free government service
  • Employers use it to verify visa conditions and work rights instantly
  • You can also check your own work rights at immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/already-have-a-visa/check-visa-details-and-conditions/check-conditions-online
  • Share your VEVO results proactively with new employers

📄 Documents for Onboarding

  • Passport — valid and not expired
  • Visa grant notification — email from Department of Home Affairs
  • Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) — shows current student status
  • Tax File Number (TFN) — apply through ATO before starting work
  • Bank account details — Australian bank account for salary payments

⚡ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Working more than 48 hours per fortnight during study — this is a visa breach
  • Starting work before your course officially starts
  • Not declaring your visa status to an employer — they find out via VEVO anyway
  • Letting your CoE lapse — always maintain enrolment if on a student visa
  • Applying for roles that require Australian citizenship without checking — saves time for both you and the employer

🏛️ Roles That Commonly Require Citizenship

  • Federal & state government jobs (APS, Services Australia, ATO, Defence) — almost always require Australian citizenship
  • Defence contractors (Leidos, BAE Systems, L3Harris) — security clearances require citizenship
  • Telco & infrastructure companies with government contracts — some tech and operations roles are citizenship-restricted
  • Major banks (certain divisions) — compliance, fraud, and government-facing teams may require citizenship or PR
  • Tip: If in doubt, email HR before applying: "Is this role open to candidates on a [visa type]?" — a quick email saves weeks of effort

📞 Getting Help

  • Department of Home Affairs: 131 881 (inside Australia)
  • International Student Welfare: your university's international office
  • Fair Work Ombudsman: 13 13 94 — for wage theft or unfair treatment
  • Migration agent: Registered Migration Agents (MARN number) for visa advice
